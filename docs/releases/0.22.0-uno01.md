# Uno Launcher 0.22.0-uno01

A faster Uno Keyboard with autocorrect, suggestions and iOS-style haptics.

## What's new in the keyboard

- **Autocorrect and suggestions.** A strip above the keys shows the word as typed, the best correction in bold, and common completions; between words it shows likely next words. Space, punctuation and Return apply the correction, and **backspace right after undoes it** (that word is then left alone). It fixes slips like `teh`, `recieve`, `occured` and `dont`, keeps your capitals, and never touches URLs, addresses, handles or words with digits. It switches itself off in password, email, web address, name and number fields.
  - It uses a bundled 80,000-word English list (frequencies from the SymSpell project, MIT, shown in the app's notices). It **never learns from what you type and saves nothing**, so it also can't learn your own names yet.
- **Faster typing.** Letters type the moment your finger lands, overlapping taps are no longer lost, the key preview no longer creates a window per press, and the keyboard makes far fewer calls into the app per keystroke (a letter costs one instead of two; a space or backspace about one instead of four or five). Suggestions are computed in the background.
- **Fixed: keys jiggling away from the one you press.** The preview pop-up was adding an invisible child to the key row and widening its gaps.
- **iOS-style haptics.** A light tick on touch-down, firmer for the space bar and modifier keys, a soft click for Return, lighter for held delete and cursor sliding, and a tick across accent choices. **Key haptics** slider (0 is off) and a **Feel it** button under Customize > Control panel & extras > Uno Keyboard.
- **FlorisBoard-style extras:** an optional number row, and sliding along the space bar to move the cursor. Each has a switch.

The keyboard is still optional, local and non-networked (a unit test forbids network, logging, file-writing and clipboard code in it). It has no swipe typing, emoji or voice input yet and is English (US) only.

## Notes: what this release has and hasn't been tested on

Tested only on an emulator that renders in software; real-hardware performance is unmeasured.

Verified on the emulator with real taps: typing "teh quick" quickly gave "the quick", backspace right after a correction restored the typed word and later left it alone, the suggestion strip showed the typed word, the bold correction and completions, and the keys stayed put while a key was held (checked by comparing pixels). The word engine was tested against the real word list, and the haptic profile, the in-memory text copy and capitalisation rules by unit test.

Please check on your Pixel:

- **Whether the haptics feel right.** I could not feel anything on an emulator. Use **Feel it** and the slider; tell me which keys feel too strong or too faint.
- **Whether autocorrect is too eager or too timid** on the words you really type, and whether typing feels faster.
- **The space-bar cursor sliding**, which I wrote but did not try on screen.
- **Top-row key preview** with the suggestion strip turned off (it would be clipped).
- Everything still unverified from earlier releases (F-Droid, Aurora and Vanadium hand-off, stacks with your widgets, the island ring at your cutout, the tilt highlight, split screen).
