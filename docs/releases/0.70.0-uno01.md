# Uno Launcher 0.70.0-uno01

Uno Actions in the search field, iOS-class sheet and page motion, haptic choreography, and a more iOS keyboard.

## What's new

- **Uno Actions.** Type a command in the island's search field and the launcher does it: `timer 10`, `stopwatch`, `note buy milk` (into your Note widget), `alarm in 20` or `alarm 7:30` (opens your clock app with the time filled in), `torch`, `focus` / `focus on` / `focus off`, and — with contacts enabled in search — `call Mom` or `text Sam` (the dialer is shown; a call is never placed for you). Every action says what it will do first, everything runs on the phone, and nothing new needs a permission.
- **iOS sheet and edge motion.** A decisive downward flick throws a sheet away, while scrolling a long sheet past its top still never closes it. The news feed rubber-bands at its ends with the classic iOS resistance curve. Home pages and the island's swipe-to-close now settle with your finger's release speed.
- **Haptic choreography.** A detent tick when a pull-down gesture crosses its threshold, a tick when a dragged icon crosses into a new cell, long-press through the haptics switch, and one centralized toggle haptic. Sounds stay off by default.
- **A smoother island.** The island's animation frames no longer allocate paths, paints or gradients — less garbage-collector pressure on the 120 Hz glass. No visual change.
- **A more iOS keyboard.** Per-key-class haptics (light letter ticks, deeper space/return clicks, a soft two-tick toggle on shift and symbol keys), a double-space-for-period switch (on by default), flick down on a letter for a comma and flick up for an apostrophe, and autocorrect that reads two words back, capitalizes sentence starts and knows more contractions. It still never learns or stores anything you type.

## Notes: what this has and hasn't been tested on

Unit tests pass (809, including new suites for the action parsers, the elastic-edge curve, the sheet-fling thresholds, the per-key haptic profiles and the autocorrect context), and the debug build installs on an Android 16 emulator. **It has not been run on a real phone**; the motion and haptic feel in this release is exactly the kind of thing only a finger can judge. See `docs/device-checklist.md`.
